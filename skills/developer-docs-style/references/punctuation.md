# Punctuation

Rules from the punctuation pages of the Google developer documentation style guide. Use standard American punctuation where this file is silent.

## Commas

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/commas

- Use the serial comma in a series of three or more: "zones, regions, and multi-regions."
- Put a comma after an introductory phrase: "Finally, only groups that contain parameters appear in this list."
- Put a comma before *and*, *but*, or *or* when it joins two independent clauses, unless both are very short: "Type your ID and click **OK**."
- Put a comma before a nonrestrictive *which*: "Name of the group, which has a maximum length of 200 characters."

## Colons

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/colons

- The text before a colon that introduces a list must be a complete sentence: "The fields are defined as follows:" not "The fields are:"
- Start the text after a colon in lowercase, unless it's a proper noun, a heading, a quotation, or text after a label such as **Note:**.

## Semicolons

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/semicolons

- Avoid semicolons where you can. Not all of them are read aloud by screen readers.
- Use one only to join closely related clauses where a period works less well, before a conjunctive adverb such as *therefore*, or between series items that have commas of their own.

## Dashes and hyphens

Sources: https://developers.google.com/style/dashes and https://developers.google.com/style/hyphens

- Use an em dash, with no spaces around it, for a break in a sentence. Don't use an en dash, or a hyphen in place of an em dash.
- Never separate an item from its description with a dash. Use a colon or a period: "Example: This is an example."
- Use a hyphen with no spaces for number ranges ("8-20 files"), or write "from 8 to 20 files." Don't mix the two.
- Don't hyphenate most prefixes: "metadata," "preprocessing." Do hyphenate *self-* and *cross-*, a prefix before a capital or a number ("non-Google," "post-2000"), and a prefix that would cause misreading ("re-sign").
- Hyphenate a compound modifier before a noun when it helps clarity ("a well-designed app"), but not after a verb ("the app is well designed"), and never after an *-ly* adverb ("publicly available").

## Parentheses

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/parentheses

- Keep important information out of parentheses, because some readers skip them.
- Don't write optional plurals such as "file(s)."
- Put the period inside the parentheses when they hold a full sentence, and outside when they close one.

## Quotation marks

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/quotation-marks

- Use quotation marks rarely outside code: for titles of short works and sections you can't link, direct quotes, and a word used in an unusual sense.
- Use straight quotation marks and apostrophes, never curly ones.
- Put commas and periods inside the closing quotation mark, except after a literal string. Better, put the string in code font: "If you enter `escape`, the program crashes."

## Periods and exclamation points

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/periods

- Don't end headings with periods.
- Avoid a period right after a URL or path. Move the URL, or put it on its own line.
- Avoid exclamation points. Never use them in concept or reference docs.

## Ampersands

Source: https://developers.google.com/style/accessibility#general-dos-and-donts

- Write *and*. Use `&` only when it's in a UI label you're quoting, in code, or where a table heading or diagram label has no room.
